This is the scoring standard behind the Proper Prep check in Deckspert. It
scores the prep only: storyboard beats (Opening Gambit, Situation, Big Idea,
WIIFM, Close, Proof Points) come next and have their own rubric, so they are
never marked as gaps here.

## How the scale works

The worksheet has two kinds of field.

**Checked, not scored (yes / no).** Audience & Title/Role and Behavioral Style
are filled in or they are not. They show as **PRESENT** or **MISSING**, with no
1 to 5 score, and never affect the quality scores.

**Scored 1 to 5 on quality.** The other six fields. The score sets the status:

| Score | Status | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 5-4 | **PRESENT** | Clearly there and doing its job. |
| 3-2 | **WEAK** | Present but underdeveloped, vague, generic, or off-target. |
| 1 | **MISSING** | Genuinely absent from the worksheet. |

A field that is present but rough is weak, never missing. Prep is pre-work, so
most fields should land at 2 or 3 on the first pass and climb on the re-run. If
nearly everything scores 1 on a first attempt, the rubric is miscalibrated, not
the attendee. Use 5, 3 and 1 as anchors: 4 when a field nearly meets the 5
anchor, 2 when it is present but clearly short of the 3 anchor.

**Brevity is not weakness.** A worksheet is filled in fast, in fragments. Judge
specificity, not length.

**Persuasive by default.** This program asks the audience for a decision that
has not been made yet, so Desired Outcome and the Reasons fields are about
**approval**. Where a cohort runs an Inform story instead (reporting on work
already done), the "yes" becomes adoption; the anchors hold either way.

## 1) THE AUDIENCE: Who Is in the Room

### 1a) Audience & Title/Role *(checked, not scored)*

**What it must do.** Say who the audience is (the person or account) and their
title or role.

| | |
|---|---|
| **PRESENT** | Both are filled in. |
| **MISSING** | Either is blank. |

### 1b) Behavioral Style *(checked, not scored)*

**What it must do.** Tick one of Thinker, Director, Socializer or Relater.

| | |
|---|---|
| **PRESENT** | One style is ticked. |
| **MISSING** | None ticked. |

Feedback names the style and one line on what it means for tailoring: Thinkers
want logic and detail, Directors want the bottom line and options, Socializers
want vision and energy, Relaters want trust and low risk.

---

## 2) THE NEEDS: What They Must Solve, for the Business and for Themselves

### 2a) Core Needs

**What it must do.** The core, department or category needs this audience must
solve.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5 STRONG** | One to three needs, each concrete and specific to this audience's function or category, and each addressed by the Desired Outcome. |
| **3 WEAK** | Needs are real but vague ("improve efficiency", "drive growth"), or so many that none stands out, or they are the presenter's needs rather than the audience's. |
| **1 MISSING** | Absent. |

**Most common failure:** Listing what the presenter needs from the room
(approval, budget) instead of what the room needs solved.

### 2b) Business Needs

**What it must do.** The commercial pressures on this audience: growth, cost,
risk, capacity.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5 STRONG** | Named pressures with a number or direction where one exists ("category down 3% while the segment grows"), clearly relevant to this audience. |
| **3 WEAK** | Pressures named in the abstract ("cost pressure", "competitive market") with nothing specific to this business. |
| **1 MISSING** | Absent. |

**Most common failure:** Restating the Core Needs with a dollar sign. Core is
what their function must fix; Business is the pressure that makes it urgent.

### 2c) Personal Needs

**What it must do.** What the decision-maker personally gains, fears, or is
measured on.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5 STRONG** | Something personal and specific: the metric on their scorecard, the commitment they made upward, the risk to their credibility, the win they want to be known for. |
| **3 WEAK** | Business needs restated in personal language ("they want the company to grow"), or a generic trait ("wants results"). |
| **1 MISSING** | Absent. |

**Most common failure:** The mirror problem. If you swap "they" for "the
company" and the sentence still works, it is a business need.

**Quick test:** *What would they be praised or blamed for?* If the field does
not answer that, it has not reached personal. Coaching only, not a scoring cap.

---

## 3) THE OUTCOME: What You Want, and Why They Would or Wouldn't

### 3a) Desired Outcome

**What it must do.** What you want the audience to decide, approve or do when
you finish.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5 STRONG** | One observable outcome, specific enough that you would know whether it happened: who decides what, and ideally by when. It answers the needs in section 2. |
| **3 WEAK** | An outcome exists but is an aspiration ("get buy-in", "raise awareness", "align on next steps"), or several outcomes compete. |
| **1 MISSING** | Absent. |

**Most common failure:** "Buy-in." Buy-in to what, shown how? The outcome
should be something you could see them do the week after.

**Carries forward:** This becomes the ask in the storyboard's Opening Gambit and
Close. A vague outcome here is usually the root of Weak Decision Framing there.

### 3b) Reasons to Say Yes

**What it must do.** Why this audience would say yes, each reason tied to a need
they have.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5 STRONG** | Two to four reasons, each traceable to a specific Core, Business or Personal need above, in the audience's terms rather than the seller's. |
| **3 WEAK** | Reasons are real but generic selling points ("proven results", "best-in-class") with no link to their needs. |
| **1 MISSING** | Absent. |

**Most common failure:** Writing features instead of reasons. A feature is true
of the offer; a reason is true for this person.

### 3c) Reasons to Say No

**What it must do.** The real objections and reasons this audience might
decline, surfaced honestly so they can be pre-empted.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5 STRONG** | Specific, honest objections this audience would actually hold, including the uncomfortable ones (cost, disruption, a past failure, a competing priority, the Personal Need at risk). |
| **3 WEAK** | Token objections ("budget", "timing") with nothing specific, or objections the presenter can already dismiss easily. |
| **1 MISSING** | Blank, or "none." |

**Most common failure:** Leaving it thin because it feels negative. The honesty
of this field is what makes the objections pre-emptable later.

## Across the whole worksheet: the connections

Not scored, but where the most useful coaching lands.

- **Needs and outcome.** The Desired Outcome answers the needs in 2a to 2c.
- **Needs and yes.** Each Reason to Say Yes traces back to a named need. A
  reason with no need behind it is a selling point.
- **No and outcome.** The Desired Outcome is still realistic given the Reasons
  to Say No. If the biggest objection would block it outright, the outcome may
  need to be smaller.
- **Style and approach.** The ticked style is reflected elsewhere: a Director's
  Desired Outcome is a crisp decision; a Thinker's Reasons to Say Yes carry
  evidence.

## Overall read

| Read | When |
|---|---|
| **needs work** | 3 or more scored fields score 1, OR the Desired Outcome is missing |
| **mixed** | Some real strengths, but 1 to 2 critical gaps or broken connections |
| **strong** | No scored field below 3, and the three Needs fields and Desired Outcome are all specific and connected |

(Changed from what is in code today, which keys "needs work" and "strong" partly
on Audience being specific. With Audience now a yes/no check, the thresholds
rest on the scored fields.)
